Care & Maintenance

🎺General Care🎺

    Wipe fingerprints from the surface of your instrument after every practice session to prevent dirt, oil, and fingerprints from building up and affecting the instruments appearance.

    Empty any moisture from inside the instrument after playing, as leaving moisture inside for long periods can cause corrosion and other problems.

    Use a soft, clean polishing cloth to gently remove fingerprints, dust, and marks without scratching or damaging the finish of the instrument.

Handling

    Always carry your instrument carefully inside its protective case when travelling or moving between rooms to reduce the risk of accidental damage.

    Never place your instrument directly on the floor because it can easily be stepped on, knocked over, or exposed to dirt and moisture.

💧Oiling Guide💧

Valve Oil

    Oil the valves every few days, or whenever they begin to feel slower than normal, so they continue moving smoothly and respond properly while you are playing.

    Use only valve oil that is specifically designed for brass instruments, as using unsuitable oils or other substances could damage the valves or affect their performance.

Slide Grease

    Apply a small amount of slide grease when the slides become stiff or difficult to move, making sure the slides can move smoothly without becoming loose or leaking air.

🫧Cleaning🫧

Daily

    Remove moisture from the instrument after every playing session to help prevent a buildup of water and reduce the chance of corrosion or other internal problems.

    Clean the mouthpiece regularly because saliva, dirt, and other particles can build up inside it and affect both hygiene and the quality of your playing.

Deep Clean

    Give the instrument a thorough wash every few months to remove dirt, oils, and buildup that cannot be removed through normal daily cleaning.

    Use warm water rather than hot water when cleaning the instrument, because extremely hot water can damage the finish, seals, or other parts of the instrument.

📦Storage📦

    Keep your instrument safely inside its protective case whenever you are not playing it to protect it from dust, accidental knocks, scratches, and other damage.

    Avoid storing the instrument in places with extreme heat, cold, or humidity because large changes in temperature and moisture can damage the instrument over time.

    Do not place heavy objects on top of the instrument case, as excessive weight could damage both the case and the instrument inside it.

🔨Repairs🔨

    Stuck valves can make the instrument difficult to play and should be checked carefully rather than being forced, as excessive pressure could cause further damage.

    Loose slides can affect the instruments tuning and performance, so they should be checked and adjusted carefully when necessary.

    Dents in the instrument can affect how easily it plays and may require professional tools and experience to repair correctly.

    Water key problems can prevent moisture from being removed properly, so damaged or loose water keys should be repaired as soon as possible.

Visit a professional repair technician if a problem cannot be safely fixed yourself, as attempting complicated repairs without the correct tools or knowledge could cause more serious damage.

✨Top Tips✨

    Practice regular cleaning habits after every rehearsal or practice session so that dirt, moisture, and fingerprints do not build up and cause problems over time.

    Keep a small bottle of suitable valve oil with your instrument so you can lubricate the valves whenever they begin to feel stiff or slow.

    Clean the mouthpiece often to maintain good hygiene and prevent dirt and buildup from affecting the way the instrument plays.

    Store your instrument safely inside its case after every rehearsal or practice session to protect it from accidental damage and keep it ready for your next use.
